What Gen 2 does

Four systems.
One routing decision.

Each layer can be inspected in the console: the feeds, the road network, the graph, and the health of every provider.

01

Live hazard feeds

Seismic events from USGS and weather from Open-Meteo are pulled per city and converted into incident severity.

  • USGS
  • Open-Meteo
02

Road-network routing

Hub-to-incident distance and time come from OSRM road data. Where none is available the map draws a dashed estimate.

  • OSRM
  • Leaflet
03

Graph optimization

Weighted shortest paths plus max-flow for capacity, with a Graph Lab showing the network the decision was made on.

  • Dijkstra
  • A*
  • Edmonds-Karp
04

Provider health

Every data provider reports status in the header, and a built-in stress test pushes the solver with synthetic load.

  • Health strip
  • Stress test
How a decision forms
Hazard+Network=Route

Live conditions are weighed against road and capacity limits, so the response path is computed rather than chosen.

Under the hood

What Gen 2 is built on

Current ResQNet
01

Dijkstra and A* routing

Risk-weighted shortest paths across a hub, incident and hospital graph, with heuristic search for speed.

Solver
02

Edmonds-Karp max-flow

Capacity-aware flow to estimate how many people the network can actually move, not just how fast a single vehicle arrives.

Capacity
03

OSRM road routing

Real road distance and time between hubs and incidents, with a marked straight-line estimate when the service is unreachable.

Routing
04

USGS and Open-Meteo feeds

Live earthquake and weather data per city, with per-provider health reported in the app header.

Data
05

Stress testing

Synthetic city and incident load to check how the solver behaves well beyond the North Bay dataset.
